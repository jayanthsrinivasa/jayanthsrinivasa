About

I'm a Senior Researcher at Cisco Research, working on AI agents that people can count on, both in software and in the physical world.

Most of my work is about making LLMs useful in practice: agents that plan and call tools, systems that hold up on real enterprise tasks, and the post-training and reinforcement learning that make models more reliable at them. Recent examples include TIER, on rewards for multi-step tool use, and Context Bootstrapped Reinforcement Learning.

More recently I've turned to embodied AI, where models that understand language and images have to act in the physical world. We run vision-language-action policies on a Mobile ALOHA robot, and with collaborators at Purdue I presented work on long-horizon robot navigation and planning at IROS 2026.

Cisco Research looks a few years ahead. We build prototypes, some kept internal and some released as open source, and bring what we learn back into Cisco's products. I work closely with academic groups at UCLA, CMU, UC San Diego and Purdue.

I've been at Cisco for 18 years, moving from hardware engineering to software, technical leadership and then research. I hold an M.S. in Electrical Engineering: Systems from the University of Michigan and a Master of Information and Data Science from UC Berkeley.

Research areas

What I work on

LLM agents and applications

Agents that plan and call tools, multi-agent systems, and serving infrastructure for agentic workloads, often tested on Cisco problems such as log analysis and network diagnosis.

  • tool use and multi-agent systems
  • agent serving
  • AI for research and data science

Post-training and RL for LLMs

Improving model performance after pretraining with reinforcement learning, including how training context and reward design affect what a model learns. Related work covers reasoning and safety.

  • RL for LLMs (CBRL, self-play RL)
  • reasoning and safety
  • evaluation

Embodied AI

A newer area: vision-language-action models for mobile manipulation, and agentic frameworks for long-horizon robot navigation and planning.

  • VLA models
  • imitation learning
  • long-horizon planning

New area

Embodied AI

Embodied AI is a newer direction for me. I'm exploring how well current vision-language-action models carry over to real hardware, and what it takes to collect data and run them reliably. The working platform is a Mobile ALOHA bimanual mobile manipulator, which we use for demonstrations and experiments.

Prototypes & open source

Selected projects

Prototypes are part of the research. Some stay internal to Cisco; others are released as open source. These are the public ones, plus the robotics testbed.

Open source

LION

Conversational log analysis. Unsupervised methods narrow large logs to the few lines that matter, then an LLM answers questions using those lines as context.

github.com/cisco-open/lion
Open source

Blaze

Builds natural language understanding pipelines from modular components, and benchmarks and compares pipelines on standard datasets.

github.com/cisco-open/Blaze
Open source

BUFFALO

An enterprise gateway for LLMs. It sits between users and models to address data privacy and security, cost from redundant queries, hallucinations and explainability.

github.com/cisco-open/buffalo
Prototype, 2019

SuperMod

Flags toxic language in a drafted email or message so the sender can revise it before sending. UC Berkeley MIDS capstone.

ischool.berkeley.edu
Prototype

Mobile ALOHA embodied AI testbed

A bimanual mobile manipulator set up for teleoperated data collection and for running imitation-learning and vision-language-action policies on real tasks.
